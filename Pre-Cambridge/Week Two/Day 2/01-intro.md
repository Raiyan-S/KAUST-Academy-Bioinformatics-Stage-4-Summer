## 2.1 Why NGS? Potential Applications

Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) has revolutionized biology by allowing us to “read” the genetic code at unprecedented speeds and low costs. This has opened doors across multiple fields:

* **Clinical Diagnostics:** Identifying the specific mutations causing rare diseases or guiding cancer treatment through personalized medicine.
* **Agricultural Science:** Breeding crops that are more resilient to climate change by identifying drought-resistance genes.
* **Evolutionary Biology:** Tracking how species adapt over time or uncovering the origins of human populations.
* **Public Health:** Sequencing viral genomes (like SARS-CoV-2) in real-time to track transmission and the rise of new variants.

## 2.2 The Analysis Pipeline

In this course, we focus on the **DNA-seq Variant Calling** workflow. Because raw data is messy and massive, we follow a strict sequence of events to ensure our final results are accurate.

### 2.2.1 The Five Pillars of the Course:

1. **Data Pre-processing:**
   * **Quality Control (QC):** Using `FastQC` to evaluate the integrity of our raw data.
   * **Data Cleaning:** Using `fastp` to trim adapters and filter out “noise.”
2. **Reporting:** Summarising outputs from the QC tools with `MultiQC`.
3. **Reference Preparation:**
   * Downloading the reference sequence (the genomic “map”) from public databases in FASTA and GTF formats.
   * Indexing the reference sequence with `SAMtools` and `BWA`.
4. **Sequence Alignment:**
   * Using `BWA-MEM 2` to align our sequence data to the reference (find where each read belongs on the map).
   * Running `SAMtools` to sort and index the results of the mapping .
5. **Variant Calling:**
   * Using `BCFtools` to call variants which pinpoint exactly where your sample differs from the reference.
   * Filtering variants so only the true variants are left to study.

## 2.3 The Endpoints: What is the “Result”?

Bioinformatics is often about data reduction. We start with very large files (often gigabytes) generated by sequencing machines and end with smaller files (a few kilobytes) that contain the information thats useful for making biological insights. Your final outputs will be:

* **The VCF File:** A list of mutations (Short Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs), insertions and deletions (Indels)) that define your sample.
* **The Quality Report:** A `MultiQC` report that shows whether your data is suitable for downstream analysis.
* **Biological Insight:** The ability to say, *“This specific mutation on Chromosome 7 is likely responsible for the observed phenotype.”*

Warning**Garbage In, Garbage Out:** The most important rule in bioinformatics:

In bioinformatics, your results are only as reliable as the data you start with. This principle is known as **Garbage In, Garbage Out**. No matter how sophisticated your variant caller is or how expensive your server might be, if your raw sequencing data is low-quality, your biological conclusions will be flawed. Always do your QC!

## 2.4 Summary

TipKey Points

* NGS Analysis is a multi-step process that converts raw, noisy signals into structured biological information.
* Reproducibility is non-negotiable; using tools like Conda ensures your science can be verified by others.
* **Garbage In, Garbage Out:** The quality of your final variant calls depends entirely on the stringency of your initial QC and trimming steps.
* While we use specific tools like `BWA` and `BCFtools`, the underlying concepts of alignment and probability apply across the entire field.
